Papers with public code and data

Joules Per Task: Energy Measurements for Learned Policies on Embedded Hardware. code
Preparing submission. Energy per successful task for a vision-language-action policy on the smallest Jetson, concurrent with a Shanghai Jiao Tong study on larger Jetsons that reports energy separately from success. Extended across four policies spanning 0.2M to 450M parameters on one Jetson. A 19M diffusion policy costs 5x a 30M transformer policy, so parameter count is a bad proxy for edge energy. Includes the vision-reuse failure boundary and six documented deployment traps.
Per-Block Drafter Selection in Speculative Decoding. code
In preparation. 7,309 blocks with four drafters verified on identical prefixes across two GPUs. Workload-level routing is a measured null at 0.6% headroom; per-block confidence selection captures 61% of a 13.3% oracle gap, and transfers to code, math and chat but carries no signal on copy. Every number has a bootstrap interval, and the wall-clock break-even condition is stated rather than assumed.
SeabedNet Churchill Corridor Report: Model-Completed Bathymetry and a Shoal-Hazard Field for the Churchill Trade Corridor. report · atlas · code and validation cells
Technical report, September 2026. A masked-completion network trained on the public CHS NONNA archive, scored on depths it never saw. Trained only on pre-2016 soundings, it predicts 6.46 million post-2016 soundings in the corridor to 13.3 m mean error against 18.8 m for the nearest published sounding and 18.1 m for the gravity prior. On 109,044 independent shelf multibeam cells: 4.9 m against 5.5 m gravity and 13.4 m nearest; below 400 m the gravity prior wins and the product defers to it. A separate shoal model predicts the shallowest point within 500 m to 2.6 m where a sounding exists (10.7 m if the archive depth is taken as the shoal). Six TSB groundings hindcast from pre-incident soundings: four fall above the 90th hazard percentile. A 1,314-cell forecast is sealed by SHA-256 and OpenTimestamps for the next survey to grade. Planning prior, not for navigation.

Achievements

  • A seabed-completion model trained on pre-2016 Canadian Hydrographic Service soundings predicts 6.46 million later soundings under the Churchill corridor to 13.3 m, against 18.1 m for the gravity-derived bathymetry that global charts fall back on; the forecast for unsounded cells is sealed and timestamped so the next survey can grade it.
  • A VLA policy measured on a Jetson Orin Nano at 963 J per successful task, cut to 489 J with no accuracy loss and no training. Energy is charged to successes, so a config that saves power by failing more pays for it.
  • Measured that inference structure, not parameter count, sets edge energy: a 19M diffusion policy burns 5x a 30M transformer policy, and the identical vision-reuse optimization returns 18.9x on one and 1.55x on the other.
  • 43,509 to 69,461 tok/s aggregate serving Llama-3.1-8B on a single B200, verified across two datacenters. 3,492 to 5,025 tok/s sustained single-stream.
  • Hochelaga-8B scores 85.44% accuracy on QFrCoLA against 83.85% for Claude Opus 4.7, both on the same 7,546-item test split of graalul/qfrcola, the Opus number measured by me through the API with the same prompt. Table and setup in the model card. First open-weights Québec-native model, trained on a corpus I assembled.
  • Full-parameter continued pretrain of a 24B on 4x B200 under FSDP2.
  • A full-duplex 7B answering a real Montréal phone number, with mid-conversation answer injection from a larger model.
  • A Québécois voice assistant live on the public web, served entirely from a DGX Spark on my desk, 0.5 s median voice latency.
  • Real neural wiring beats degree-matched random graphs as a reservoir computer: R² 0.759 ± 0.014 vs 0.612 ± 0.047, winning 10 of 10 seeds.
  • Typed system operations cut an agent's tool round-trips from 3 to 1 and its token cost by 3.1x to 3.7x, on a small two-task benchmark.
  • Three published-paper reproductions on a 15 W Jetson with added energy measurements.
